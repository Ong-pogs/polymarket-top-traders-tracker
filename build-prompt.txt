Build me a local, read-only "top traders tracker" for Polymarket that uses the Predictefy API. It reads Polymarket's profit leaderboards, opens each top wallet's positions, and shows what those wallets hold right now. It must never place orders and never ask for a wallet key.

STACK
- Node.js 22 or newer, ES modules, no npm dependencies. Use the built-in http server, fetch and node:test.
- Files: bot/engine.mjs (pure functions, no network), bot/server.mjs (refresh, saved snapshots, local web server), bot/public/index.html (one-file dashboard), bot/engine.test.mjs, bot/peek.mjs, .env.example, README.md.
- Read PREDICTEFY_API_KEY from the process environment or a local .env file (parse it with node:util parseEnv). Treat the placeholder value in .env.example as "not set" and exit with a clear message. Send the key only in the Authorization: Bearer header, only from the Node process. Never send it to the browser, never log it.
- Bind the web server to 127.0.0.1 only. Default port 8818, overridable with TRACKER_PORT.

DATA (base URL https://data.predictefy.com, all GET)
- Leaderboards: /v1/traders/polymarket/leaderboard?by=profit&window=week|month|all&limit=N (limit up to 100, 1 credit each). Rows have wallet, displayName, rank, profitUsd, volumeUsd. Take 15 wallets from the week board and 25 each from the month and all-time boards by default. A wallet on several boards is one trader with a rank on each.
- Open positions: /v1/accounts/polymarket/{wallet}/positions?limit=100&cursor=... (3 credits a page; follow nextCursor). Normalized fields are at the top level: marketId, outcomeId, side, size, avgEntryPrice, markPrice, unrealizedPnl, realizedPnl, status, asOf. The market's title, outcome, slug, eventSlug, endDate, redeemable and negativeRisk are under raw. Rows come back ordered by share count, largest first, so worthless old positions come first: keep paging, up to 4 pages a wallet by default, and keep only rows worth $1 or more (size x markPrice). If a cursor is left after the last page read, label the wallet "partial" and remember the last row's share count: every unread position is no bigger than that.
- Copy check, only when the user asks for one: /api/polymarket/fetchOrderBook?outcomeId=...&limit=50 (5 credits) and /api/polymarket/fetchOHLCV?outcomeId=...&resolution=1h&start=...&end=...&limit=1000 for the last 7 days (5 credits). Both take the position's outcomeId.
- Count the x-credits-charged response header on every response, by kind of request, and show the totals.
- Pace requests: at most 50 a minute by default (the free plan allows 60). On 429, wait for the Retry-After seconds and retry, at most 3 times.
- On 402, or 403 with code PLAN_REQUIRED, stop the refresh, say in plain words what happened, and show a link to https://predictefy.com/en/pricing in the terminal and in a banner on the dashboard.

BEHAVIOUR
- On start, refresh if there is no saved snapshot or the newest is older than the refresh interval (24 hours by default), then check once a minute whether a refresh is due. `--once` refreshes one time and exits. TRACKER_REPLAY=1 serves the saved snapshots and reads nothing.
- Save every refresh as its own file under data/snapshots/, with the time it was taken, the boards, each trader's kept positions, and the requests and credits it used.
- Netting: group a wallet's positions by marketId. If it holds two different outcomes of one market, the smaller side is "locked": one share of each pays $1 whatever happens, so those pairs are not a bet. Net size is the larger side minus the smaller; net value is net size x that side's mark price; locked value is the pair count x (the two mark prices added). Show both. Settled positions waiting to be cashed in (raw.redeemable) are listed separately and never counted as open.
- A trader's "at risk" is the sum of net values in unsettled markets. "Nothing open" means under $1 at risk and under $1 locked.
- Agree: outcomes where two or more tracked wallets each hold a net position of $5,000 or more (configurable). Disagree: markets where different tracked wallets hold different net outcomes at that size. Both are worked out after netting, so a both-sides wallet never counts as a holder.
- Copy check: walk the asks to price a buy of $1,000 (configurable). Report the best ask, the average fill price, the shares, and whether the book was deep enough: never report a full quote the book cannot fill. Each share pays $1 if the outcome happens, so compare what is left to win: 1 minus their average entry against 1 minus your average price, and the return on stake for each. If your price is lower than theirs, say that the market has moved against them, not that it is a bargain. A position row has an average price and no date: say so next to the price chart.
- What changed: compare the newest snapshot with the one before it, wallet by wallet, after netting. Report opened, added, reduced, switched sides, gone (sold or settled: the two cannot be told apart) and settled-but-unclaimed, for changes of $5,000 or more. Also report when a both-sides pile grows or shrinks even though the net bet is still nothing. A partial read can hide a small position: mark a change "unsure" when it depends on one, except that a position larger than the last row read is provably gone.
- Cost: show the credits of the last refresh, the monthly total at the current interval against the free plan's 25,000 credits, and how often each plan (Free 25,000, Builder 500,000, Pro 5,000,000 credits a month) could refresh.

DASHBOARD (dark theme, large type, readable on a screen recording)
- Header with the name, the line "What Polymarket's most profitable wallets are holding right now. A record, not a signal.", the data source, the snapshot time and the refresh status.
- Overview row: what the all-time top 10 made against what they have at risk now, drawn as two bars on one scale; wallets tracked; how many hold nothing; dollars on both sides; credits used by the last refresh. Name the endpoint each number came from.
- Tabs: Leaderboard (switch between week, month and all time), Agree & disagree, What changed, What this costs (with the settings), What it can't tell you.
- Leaderboard rows: rank, public handle, shortened wallet, profit labelled as Polymarket's figure, at risk now, a stacked bar of at-risk against both-sides dollars, position count, biggest position, and badges for "nothing open", "both sides" and "partial read".
- Clicking a wallet opens its book: at risk, on both sides, paper result, rows read; then each open position after netting with shares, a paid-to-now marker on a 0 to $1 track, value, paper result and a "Copy check" button. Label both-sides rows and rows in multi-outcome events (raw.negativeRisk), where the average entry can mislead.
- Copy check view: their average entry, the best ask, your average for the chosen size; a bar showing where the dollar stands; the order book ladder; an hourly price chart with their average entry as a dashed line.
- Shorten wallet addresses everywhere, and shorten default handles that are just an address. Escape every string that comes from an API before putting it in the page.
- The limits tab says, in plain words: a wallet is not a person; profit is Polymarket's number; big wallets are read in part; average entry can mislead in multi-outcome events; it shows what they hold, not when they will sell; "gone" is not always "sold".

ALSO
- bot/peek.mjs prints one raw leaderboard row (`node bot/peek.mjs board`) or one raw position row for a handle on the boards (`node bot/peek.mjs book <handle>`), with the wallet address shortened.
- Unit tests for the pure functions, including: a wallet on several boards becomes one trader; equal Yes and No holdings net to nothing at risk; an uneven pair leaves the difference on the larger side; settled positions are not counted as open; agreement needs two wallets after netting and a both-sides wallet does not count; a thin order book is not reported as a full quote; the copy check's returns; opened, added, reduced, gone and settled changes; a shrinking both-sides pile is reported; a partial read marks small changes unsure; one refresh a day at about 280 credits fits the free plan.
- README with quick start, a "what this costs to run" table, the endpoints used, the settings, and a plain list of what the tool cannot tell you.
